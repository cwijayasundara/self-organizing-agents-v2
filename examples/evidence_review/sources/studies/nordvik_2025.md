# Nordvik Institute (2025). "Thirty-two hours: a field trial of the four-day week"

Published in the Journal of Workplace Studies, vol. 41, peer reviewed. Funded by the national research council; the authors declare no conflicts of interest.

## Design

Randomised field trial. 38 firms (19 treated, 19 control), 2,140 employees, all in office-based knowledge work (software, finance, consulting). Duration 12 months. Contracted hours fell from 40 to 32 per week at the same pay. Average hours actually worked fell from 38.6 to 36.3 per week, because many staff kept working part of the fifth day.

## Results (treated vs control, 12 months)

| Measure | Change |
|---|---|
| Output per hour actually worked | +5.9% |
| Output per employee | -0.4% (not statistically significant) |
| Sick days per employee | -18% |
| Voluntary attrition (annual) | 14% to 10% |
| Self-reported burnout (0-10) | 6.2 to 4.9 |

## Limits

All firms were office-based; no customer-facing shift teams took part. The authors caution against applying the results to roles that require fixed hours of coverage.
